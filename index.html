A Tuesday with Arrivena

  1. A call comes in

    A coach takes a call from a county referral. Before hanging up, the client already exists in Arrivena: name, age band, referral source, callback number. The rest is a draft for later.

  2. Same call, minutes later

What it does

Arrivena follows a client from first contact through outcome: intake, a guided and revocable consent ceremony, a de-identified referral to a partner organization, placement tracking, and funding entries that export for grant reporting. Each step is recorded in the system that produced it, not written up afterward from memory. Access to any of it, across organizations, is gated on consent and partnership status, re-checked on every request.

The product, by the numbers

A facilitator without a system like this tracks a placement by phone call, keeps the case in a spreadsheet, and re-types the numbers by hand into a state funding form each quarter. Arrivena keeps the same information in one system: entered once, gated automatically by consent and partnership status, and exported straight into the file a funder expects.

How it treats confidentiality

Access to a client's record across organizations is gated on consent and partnership status, and that check is evaluated at the time of the request, not cached from an earlier session. A request for a record that a user is not authorized to see, and a request for a record that does not exist, receive the same response, so an unauthorized query cannot be used to learn whether a person is in the system. Every disclosure, referral, and placement is written to an append-only ledger, so the history of who accessed or acted on a record cannot be edited after the fact.

Commercial posture

Arrivena is sold on a flat subscription. There is no per-placement or per-referral fee, by design: a pricing model tied to referral volume creates federal kickback exposure under EKRA, and Arrivena refuses that model rather than manage the exposure after the fact. No payment processing lives anywhere in the platform. Arrivena is not an electronic health record and stores no treatment records — it tracks the administrative and financial-need workflow around a referral, not the clinical care itself.
